# DM Companion — wireframe spec

A desktop app that helps a Dungeon Master prepare and run D&D sessions at the table.
These wireframes define **layout, navigation and behaviour**, not final visual design.
Open the `.html` files in a browser (all sidebar links work), or see `images/` for screenshots.

## Context

- One DM, playing **in person with two screens**: the DM's laptop (main window) and a TV or second monitor facing the players (player display).
- The ruleset is not fixed yet. Rules data, monsters and encounter difficulty thresholds must come from a swappable data source and a per-campaign ruleset setting.
- Lore lives in the DM's **LegendKeeper** world. The app only **reads** it, never edits it.

## Start screen (no wireframe)

- The first screen when the app opens. It has no sidebar.
- **App name** at the top, then a **prominent search bar**. Once search works, it searches rules, monsters, spells and lore across every campaign.
- **Campaigns**: your campaigns, most recently opened first, each with when it was last opened. Picking one opens its Landing screen.
- **New campaign**: a name field and a Create button. Names are required, at most 100 characters, and unique (ignoring case). Creating opens the new campaign.
- **Characters** and **Library** buttons sit at the top right, in that order.
- The Settings gear sits in the bottom-left corner, and the app version is shown small at the bottom.
- The Settings gear is on every screen, always in the bottom-left corner: fixed there on the Start screen and the app-wide Characters and Library pages, and at the bottom of the sidebar inside a campaign.
- Empty state: "No campaigns yet." Campaign folders that can't be read are listed in a warning and left untouched.

## Library (no wireframe)

- Opened from a **Library** button at the top right of the Start screen. It replaces the screen (same window); **Back** returns to the Start screen. Where you are is kept in the address, so back/forward work inside the Library.
- **Reading** tab, three columns:
  - **Library**: the imported books, alphabetical (ignoring punctuation and a leading "the", "a" or "an"), with a filter field that narrows them by name or abbreviation.
  - **Contents**: the book's chapters ("Chapter 1: …", "Appendix A: …") with their sections nested and collapsible. Clicking a chapter opens it; clicking a section opens its chapter and scrolls to it.
  - **Reading pane**: the chapter text with headings, lists, tables, insets, quotes and the stat blocks books embed (loaded as they scroll into view). Images show when they were imported in Settings; nothing is fetched from the internet.
- **Catalog** tab:
  - **Books** column: every source with entries, each with a checkbox, plus a filter field (by name or abbreviation), Select all / Deselect all (acting on the books the filter shows) and a shown/total count. Unticked books are hidden from the Catalog only and remembered app-wide.
  - **Tabs**: All, Rules (rules glossary, actions), Player (spells, classes, subclasses, species, backgrounds, feats, optional features), Reference (items, item properties, item masteries, conditions, diseases, languages, skills, senses) and DM Tools (creatures, hazards, traps). Each tab has its categories as sub-tabs; All lists everything with a Category column.
  - Each category is a **table** with its own columns (e.g. spells: Level, School, Cast Time, Range, Components, Tags; creatures: CR, Type, Size, AC, HP; items: Type, Rarity, Attunement, Weight, Value), always starting with Name and ending with Source (the book's full name). Every column sorts both ways; text sorts ignore punctuation and a leading "the", "a" or "an".
  - A **search** box with a result count, and **filters** as checkbox groups per category (e.g. spell Level, School, Concentration/Ritual; creature CR range, Type, Size; item Category, Rarity, Attunement). Options within a filter combine with OR, filters with AND. Filters with a single option are hidden.
  - 50 rows per page with Previous / Next and "Showing 1–50 of N".
  - Clicking a row opens the entry on its own page with "Back to <category>"; the table keeps its search, filters, sort and page.
- **References** in the text (a spell, creature, condition…) open a **preview card** next to them. References inside the card can be followed, with Back; Escape, ✕ or clicking outside closes it and returns focus.
- Entries show a summary for their type: spells (level, school, casting time, range, components, duration), creatures (size, type, alignment, AC, HP, speed, ability scores, saves, skills, senses, languages, CR, then traits, actions, bonus actions, reactions, legendary actions and spellcasting), items (type, rarity, attunement), feats (prerequisite) and classes (hit die, primary ability, features by level).
- Empty state: "No rules data yet", with a button that opens Settings on Rules data.

## Campaign settings (no wireframe)

- A dialog for one campaign, opened from the cog next to the sidebar's campaign switcher or from a campaign's cog in Settings → Campaigns. One scrolling page:
  - **Name**: shown at the top; **Rename** edits it inline (Enter saves, Escape cancels; same name rules as creating).
  - **Status**: Active with **Archive**, or Archived with **Restore**. Archiving the open campaign returns to the Start screen.
  - **Sourcebooks**: checkboxes for every imported book, a search field that filters them by name or abbreviation, Select all / Deselect all (acting on the books shown) and a selected/total count; changes save immediately. New campaigns start with **no** sourcebooks. An archived campaign's books can't be changed until it's restored.
  - **Delete permanently**: for active and archived campaigns alike, after a "This can't be undone" confirmation. Deleting the open campaign returns to the Start screen.

## Campaign library

- A **Campaign library** item in the campaign sidebar opens the same Reading and Catalog screens inside the campaign shell, showing only the campaign's sourcebooks. With no sourcebooks selected it says so, with a **Choose sourcebooks** button that opens Campaign settings.
- A preview card for an entry from a book the campaign doesn't use still opens, with a banner: "From <book>, which isn't active for <campaign>." **Add it to this campaign** activates that book for the campaign immediately; the banner disappears and the card stays open.

## App shell (all DM screens inside a campaign)

- **Left sidebar**
  - App name: clicking it goes back to the Start screen (also in the narrow-window top bar).
  - Campaign switcher: shows the current campaign; clicking it opens a dropdown of the active campaigns, and picking one opens it. The current campaign has a green check. Every dropdown in the app (filters, sort, settings, wizard choices) has this switcher's look: a bordered button, the value on the left, an up-down chevron on the right; its open list looks like the switcher's menu too (rounded panel with a shadow, grey hover, a green check on the right of the picked value) wherever the system allows styling it (Windows), and is the system list elsewhere. A cog wheel next to it opens **Campaign settings** for this campaign.
  - Navigation: Home, Campaign library, Run encounter, Encounter builder, Characters, LegendKeeper. Search is not a sidebar item.
  - Player-screen status box at the bottom, with a "Blank screen" button.
  - A gear button in the bottom-left corner opens **Settings** as a modal dialog. Escape, the close button or clicking outside closes it. Settings has a side panel listing its sections:
    - **Theme**: an Appearance dropdown with System, Light and Dark. System is the default and follows the operating system live. The choice is saved app-wide (not per campaign) and applies from the very first paint. The player display always stays dark. An **Animations** dropdown (System, On, Off) controls all app animations (drawer, menus, card reordering, scrolling to a section); System follows the operating system's reduce-motion setting.
    - **Campaigns**: an **Add campaign** field (the new campaign is added to the list; Settings stays open), and the active and archived campaigns, each with a cog wheel that opens its **Campaign settings**.
    - **Rules data**: import 5etools-format rules data from a `.tar.gz` on your computer (the app never downloads book content). Import processes it into a local library; a failed import keeps the previous data. Each import first checks that the file is the right kind (rules data vs. images) and stops within seconds with a clear message if not. Shows the installed version and import date. **Updates**: you enter a GitHub `owner/repo`; **Check for updates** compares its latest release version with yours and tells you to import the newer `.tar.gz`. **Images** are optional, imported from a second `.tar.gz` and removable. **Books and sources** is a filterable table of everything imported (name, abbreviation, number of entries), grouped, with a checkbox per source and per group; **Remove selected** removes them in one confirmed step. Removed sources are listed separately and skipped on future imports until **Allow again**. The Settings dialog has a fixed size; only the section content scrolls. An attribution line credits Wizards of the Coast.
- **Global search**: a search bar is always visible at the top of every DM screen and stays in place while the content scrolls. It has no keyboard shortcut.
- Search results, stat blocks and LegendKeeper pages can be opened in **tabs** (see the Search screen).
- The layout must stay usable on a small laptop. When the window gets narrow, the sidebar hides behind a menu toggle in a top bar and opens as a drawer over the content. Escape, clicking outside, or navigating closes it.

### 00 · Landing
- **Header**
  - Campaign name and the next session (number, date and time).
  - A link to last session's recap.
  - Buttons: **Start session** (primary, opens Run encounter and the player display) and **Prep mode**.
- **Search**: uses the global search bar at the top (see App shell), with recent searches as chips underneath.
- **Cards** (always editable: each card has one ⋯ menu with Move left, Move right and Remove. Hovering a card shows a drag handle next to the ⋯ menu; drag it to reorder the card; the other cards slide out of the way, and Escape cancels. Removing shows "… removed · Undo". A dashed "+ Add card" tile at the end of the grid adds removed cards back. Each card appears at most once. The order is saved app-wide.)
  - Prepared encounters, each with a "Run" button.
  - Party snapshot (AC, passive Perception).
  - LegendKeeper pinned and recently viewed pages.
  - Notes for the next session.
  - Continue where you left off.
  - Quick tools: dice, conditions reference, random NPC name, blank the player screen.

### 01 · Global search
- **One query searches across** rules, spells, monsters, items and LegendKeeper lore.
- **Filter chips** by type, each showing its result count. Source and sort dropdowns.
- **Results list**, grouped by type, shown next to a **preview pane**.
- **Preview pane**
  - Key stats as a grid.
  - The full text, with inline links to the rules it references.
  - Rollable dice, for example "Roll 8d6" with a slot-level picker.
  - Actions: Pin to session, Open in new tab, Show on player screen.
  - "Mentioned in your world": LegendKeeper pages that reference this result.
- **Tabs** above the content keep several lookups open at once.

### 02 · Run encounter
- **Header**
  - Encounter name, with a link to its LegendKeeper page.
  - Round counter and timer.
  - Buttons: Previous, **Next turn** (Space), + Combatant, End encounter.
- **Initiative column**: one row per combatant.
  - Each row shows initiative, name, PC tag, HP bar, HP and AC, and condition tags with durations.
  - Concentration is shown as a tag.
  - A downed PC shows its death saves.
  - The active turn is highlighted.
- **Stat block** of the active combatant.
  - Every modifier, attack and damage value is **clickable to roll**.
  - DM notes per monster.
- **Target panel**
  - Damage, Heal and Temp HP buttons with an amount field.
  - Condition toggles, plus custom conditions.
  - A duration picker; durations count down per round.
- **Dice roller**
  - Buttons for d4–d20 and d%.
  - A free expression field (e.g. `2d20kh1+4`).
  - A roll log.

### 03 · Encounter builder
- **Monster browser**
  - Search, plus filters for CR, type and environment.
  - Add buttons; hover a monster to preview it.
  - Homebrew monsters are included.
- **Roster**
  - Quantity steppers for each monster.
  - Start options: average or rolled HP, group identical monsters on one initiative, show monster names to players.
  - Setup notes.
  - A linked LegendKeeper page.
- **Party**: checkboxes for which PCs take part.
- **Difficulty meter**: base XP, multiplier, adjusted XP and the next threshold. It follows the campaign's ruleset.
- **Buttons**: Save draft, and **Start encounter**, which loads the encounter into Run encounter.
- The sidebar lists saved encounters.

### 04 · Characters
- **Characters overview** (no wireframe), opened from the Start screen's **Characters** button. It replaces the screen; **Back** returns to the Start screen.
  - Inside a campaign, the sidebar's **Characters** item opens the same overview within the campaign shell, showing only that campaign's characters: no Campaign filter and no sort by campaign, "No characters in this campaign yet." when empty, and **Create character** starts with this campaign selected. A character sheet opened from here returns to it with **Back**.
  - Every character from every campaign (active and archived), and those without a campaign, as a card sorted by name. A character belongs to at most one campaign and is stored in that campaign's folder; one without a campaign is stored app-wide and its card says "No campaign".
  - Above the cards: a **search** field (matches the name or the class line, subclass included), **filters** for Campaign, Class (a multiclassed character matches each of its classes) and Level (total level), each offering only values some character has, and **Sort by** Name, Campaign or Level (ties go by name). When nothing matches, a message with a **Clear search and filters** button.
  - A card shows the avatar, name, class with subclass ("Fighter (Champion)", or "Fighter 3 (Champion) / Wizard 2" when multiclassed), total level, and the campaign (tagged "Archived" when it is).
  - Avatar: the character's own picture (picked under Basic info); when none was added, the imported art for the subclass, else the class, else a neutral person icon. The same avatar shows on the sheet.
  - The card's "…" menu copies the character into another active campaign (as a new, separate character): **Copy to <campaign>** when there's one other active campaign, a **Copy to** submenu listing them when there are several, and no copy item when there's none. **Delete…** removes it for good after a confirmation on the card ("Delete <name>? This can't be undone." with Cancel and Delete). A character in an archived campaign can't be deleted until the campaign is restored.
  - Clicking a card (or its name, from the keyboard) opens the character's **sheet**.
- **Create character** (no wireframe): a seven-step wizard in a large modal over the overview. A stepper shows the steps; **Back** and **Next** sit at the top right of each step, Next is enabled once the step is complete, steps already reached can be revisited, and changing an earlier choice clears the choices that depend on it. Closing (Escape or the close button) with unsaved input asks first ("Discard this character?"). Required fields are marked with *.
  1. **Basic info**: on the left an **Avatar**: a picture picked from the computer (Choose a picture / Change picture; a red ✕ on the picture's top corner removes it). A freshly picked picture can be dragged (or moved with the arrow keys) to choose which square part is the avatar; it's then cropped to that square and downsized (256 px, WebP) so it's kept in the character file; the picked file itself is kept full size beside the character's file (PNG, JPG, WebP or GIF, at most 20 MB; copying the character copies it, deleting or removing the picture deletes it). Without one the class art is used. On the right name (required, at most 100 characters), player and campaign. An (i) next to Campaign explains that it limits steps 2–4 to its sourcebooks; "No campaign" offers every imported book.
  2. **Species**: picked in two stages. **First** a grid of large cards with a name filter (name or book, abbreviation or full name): one card per option: reprints of it in other books (the rules data's `reprintedAs`, followed through books the campaign doesn't use) are its versions, and the card shows the newest one the campaign allows, with a "+2 versions" badge when there are more; options that only share a name (Aven in two settings) keep their own cards. Each card shows the entry's imported picture (its initial on a tinted panel when there's none, or no images are imported), the name, the book's full name and a few facts to compare (size, speed, darkvision and other movement). **Then**, after clicking a card, an overview replaces the grid: the picture large on the left; next to it the name with a **← Change species** link (no underline) beside it, back to the grid (the current pick stays highlighted), the book's full name (a **Version** dropdown of the books instead, each with its publication year unless the name already has one, like "Volo's Guide to Monsters (2016)", when the option has several printings; switching clears the choices that depend on it, like picking another card), the facts as chips and the choices (lineage, subspecies for older books, size), without a frame. Under them the full rules text, always shown, without a heading. Coming back to a step that already has a pick opens its overview. The choices also hold what the species itself asks (besides skills and ability bonuses, which have their own steps): a feat where it grants one (XPHB Human: an Origin feat, with that feat's own choices), its lineage's spells (preset by the lineage; spells that come at character level 3 or 5 are listed with that level and show on the sheet from then on), and tool choices. The Background step likewise asks the background's tool choices and its feat's choices (Skilled's three skills or tools, Magic Initiate's spells with the spell list the background names). A step isn't done until these are made.
  3. **Starting class** and 4. **Background**: the same two stages. Class cards show the hit die, primary ability and saving throws; background cards the abilities it raises, its skills and its feat. Sidekick classes aren't offered. The Starting class step also asks for the **Level** (1–20, default 1), with a **Configure** button next to it that opens the levels panel (the same as Edit → Levels) on the character being made, to make each level's choices now; until the Abilities step is done, feats with an ability score prerequisite show "(check prerequisite)". Levels left unconfigured get the fixed hit points and can be filled in later under Edit → Levels. The choices are applied when the character is created (or saved, when editing); picking another class clears them, and a lower level drops the ones above it. When the entry has starting equipment options, a required **Starting equipment** choice shows with the other choices (Option A: the items and gold; Option B: gold…; anything that comes with every option is listed below). On creating, the picked items go into the inventory, looked up in the rules data for weight, armor and attacks (same item from class and background is one line; one not in the library is kept by name; "any musical instrument" style entries are added by that name), and the gold goes into the coins. Editing doesn't ask for or add starting equipment.
  5. **Abilities**: a Method dropdown: Standard array (15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8), Manual (3–18) or Roll (4d6, drop the lowest, six times, through the dice parser; the dropped die is shown struck through). The six abilities are cards in one row: the score large, bonuses included (a number field for Manual, a value select underneath for array and rolls, one value per ability), and the modifier and saving throw as soon as that ability has a value. Then the bonuses: from the background (2024: +2/+1 or +1/+1/+1 among its abilities) and from the species (older books), each bonus to a different ability; scores can't go above 20.
  6. **Skills**: skills from the background and species are listed as already proficient; then the class's choice and any species or background choice, as toggle chips. A skill can't be picked twice. Then **Languages**: every character knows Common and two standard languages; languages the species or background give count toward those two (shown as given), the rest are picked from the standard languages in the imported list, and **Add a language** adds extra ones. The step is called "Skills & languages".
  7. **Review**: every choice (the species named by its chosen version, e.g. "Dragonborn (Black)" or "Elf (Drow Lineage)"), the same ability cards, plus saving throws, proficiency bonus, level 1 hit points, skill bonuses, passive Perception and the background's feat. **Create character** saves it and opens the new character's sheet.
  - Characters start at level 1. Equipment, spells, languages, tools, feat choices, subclass, avatar and alignment come later.
  - States: no rules data imported, a campaign without sourcebooks (with a Choose sourcebooks button), empty filter results, and save errors.
  - States: loading, "No characters yet.", a load error with Retry, and a warning listing character files that couldn't be read (left untouched).
- **Character sheet** (`/characters/<id>`, with `?campaign=<id>` for a character in a campaign). Layout follows `Character Sheets/HeaderB.dc.html`. It replaces the screen, and the Settings gear sits in the bottom-left corner.
  - **Top bar**: on the left **Back** to the Characters list it was opened from (the app-wide one or a campaign's), a dropdown that switches to another character of the same campaign (or another character without a campaign), styled like the campaign switcher (labelled "<Campaign> party", or "Unassigned characters" for a character without a campaign, with up/down arrows; a green check on the open character; last, **+ New character** opens the creation wizard set to the same campaign, disabled in an archived one, and goes to the new character's sheet), **Edit** (a menu: **Character** and **Levels**), and the save status (a green dot for Saved, a grey dot for Unsaved changes, a spinner while saving); on the right **Short rest**, **Long rest** and a green **Level up** button with an up arrow. Level up opens a menu with the current class ("Fighter") and **Multiclass** (both disabled at level 20 or when read-only); Multiclass does nothing yet. Nothing on the sheet rolls dice yet except spending hit dice and level-up hit points.
    - **Level up** (the class item) opens a popup, "Level up: Fighter 3 → 4", in the style of D&D Beyond: a card per thing the level brings, a **Choice needed** badge (red) on each card that still needs a decision and **Done** (green) once made, and the green **Level up to 4** button at the bottom, enabled when every decision is made (Cancel and Escape close without changes).
      - **Hit points**: Average (half the hit die + 1, plus CON), **Roll 1d{die}** through the dice parser, or **Manual roll** (type what a real die showed, 1 to the die's size), each plus CON; at least 1. Maximum and current hit points go up by the gain.
      - **Features**: every class feature of the new level (then the subclass's) with its full rules text from the rules data. The decisions sit inside the card of the feature that asks for them:
        - **Subclass** at the class's subclass level: the same picture cards and overview as the creation steps (art from the imported images, reprints as versions), but shaped for subclass art, which is tall (most about 2:5): narrower cards with 1:2 picture frames cropped around the center, and a 1:2 picture in the overview, with the subclass's rules text (its Features) in the column to the right of the picture; a picture of a very different shape (a landscape scene) is shown whole on a blurred copy of itself. Its features at this level then appear, and its always-prepared spells are listed.
        - **Ability Score Improvement**: a feat dropdown with Ability Score Improvement first, then every General feat the character qualifies for (level, ability scores and other feats are checked; feats with other prerequisites show "(check prerequisite)"), not ones already taken unless repeatable, reprints shown once (newest allowed book). **Fighting Style** and **Epic Boon** levels list their own category. The picked feat's text shows, then its own choices: its ability increase (Ability Score Improvement: +2 to one, or +1 to two), skill proficiencies, expertise, and spells (Magic Initiate: first which class's list, then its cantrips and level 1 spell; fixed spells are listed).
        - Features with options (Divine Order, Primal Order, Blessed Strikes, Elemental Fury): pick one, each with its text.
        - **Expertise**: pick that many proficient skills (count from the text).
      - Cards of their own: **invocations, metamagic, maneuvers** (how many the class or subclass table adds; one dropdown per pick, like D&D Beyond, with the picked one's text and its own choices under it: Pact of the Tome's cantrips and rituals, Lessons of the First Ones' Origin feat and that feat's choices, the known cantrip Agonizing Blast or Eldritch Spear applies to; spells an invocation grants go to the Spells tab; a Repeatable invocation can be taken again, on another target; prerequisites as for feats, including pacts and known spells), **new cantrips** and **new spells** from the class list up to the highest spell level the new level can cast (as many as the class table adds at that level; third casters use the Wizard list), and **Weapon Mastery** (the class table's count; weapons with a mastery property, shown next to it).
      - Every list has a filter when long, shows "n of N chosen", and an (i) button opens the rules preview. In every spell and cantrip list (class, feat, background, species), spells the character already has, or gets at this level (always-prepared class and subclass spells), are shown ticked and greyed out, with the class level (or the feat or species) they came from just before the (i); when an earlier level is changed under Edit → Levels, a spell a later level picked only shows that level ("Paladin 3") before the (i) and stays pickable; a spell the character gets from more than one place (picked at Paladin 1 and always prepared from Paladin 2) shows a red (!); hovering or clicking it lists every place it came from. A feat or trait that lets the player choose the spellcasting ability (Magic Initiate: Intelligence, Wisdom or Charisma) asks for it with a required dropdown; the ability is saved with those spells and the Spells tab shows it after their origin ("Magic Initiate · WIS"). A background's feat shows its full text in a collapsed section above its choices. Each spell shows a small tag for its casting time: A (action), BA (bonus action) or R (reaction); longer casting times get none. Lists only offer the campaign's sourcebooks. Spell lists come from the rules data; data imported before they existed asks for a re-import.
      - Afterwards a notification says "Thalia is now level 4" and the sheet shows the results.
    - **Edit → Character** opens the Create character wizard filled in with the character ("Edit character"; the last step saves with **Save changes**, then the sheet reloads and a notification confirms it). Every step can be opened straight from the stepper, and **Save changes** sits next to Next on every step (enabled once no step needs changes). When a change clears choices in another step (a new species asks for its skills again, a new background for its ability bonuses…), that step gets a red dot in the stepper until it's complete again; while creating, a step already passed gets the dot the same way. Basic info, species, class, background, abilities and skills can be changed; the campaign can't (use Copy to on the Characters page). Closing with changes asks first.
      - The character file keeps results, not where they came from, so editing rebuilds what it can: skill picks are spread back over the choice groups, standard-array scores get their assignment back, rolled scores come back as six values (without their dice), and the ability bonuses are kept as saved totals ("Your saved ability bonuses are kept…", with **Choose again**).
      - The Species, Class and Background steps show what changing them does. Species: its skill choices and (where it gives them) ability bonuses are asked for again, and size, lineage and subspecies are chosen again. Class: saving throws, the class's skill choices and maximum hit points change and spent spell slots reset; check a hand-set armor class. Background: its ability bonuses and skill choices are asked for again and its feat is replaced. As in creation, changing one of these clears the choices that depend on it.
      - Saving keeps everything from play (current damage, temporary hit points, death saves, conditions, inventory, coins, notes, inspiration, concentration). When the maximum hit points change, the damage already taken carries over. Keeping the class keeps its subclass.
      - What levels added (see Edit → Levels) is rebuilt from the level records: it stays with the class, a lower level drops the records above it, and a new class drops them all. The Class step's warning says so.
    - **Edit → Levels** opens a popup "Levels of Thalia": a sidebar lists every class level ("Fighter 1" … "Fighter 5", each with a one-line summary of what it picked, or "Nothing recorded yet"; a red dot marks a level that still needs a choice; arrow keys move between them); it opens on the first such level, else level 1. The right side shows that level exactly like the Level up popup, filled in with what was picked, and everything can be changed: hit points (not at level 1, which is the hit die's maximum plus CON), subclass, feats with their choices, options, invocations, spells, weapon masteries, expertise. Level 1 has choices too (Fighting Style, weapon masteries, cantrips and spells, expertise, Divine Order…). The highest level (not level 1) has a red **Remove level n** button on the left of the footer; after a confirmation ("Remove level 5?"), the character drops to the level below and everything that level gave goes with it, its subclass too. In the wizard's Configure it also lowers the Level field.
      - A level without a record (a character created above level 1, or leveled before levels were recorded) shows its decisions empty to choose now, with the fixed hit points.
      - **Save level N** (enabled when something changed) stores the level and goes through the later ones: picks that no longer fit are removed (another subclass's features after changing the subclass; an invocation whose pact is gone; a feat whose prerequisite isn't met), and those levels get the red dot. The notification says which ("Level 3 saved. Levels 7 need a choice again.").
      - Leaving a level or closing with unsaved changes asks first ("Discard the changes to level 4?"). In an archived campaign the popup is view-only.
      - Each level is stored as a record (its hit points as average or the die result, rolled or typed as a manual roll; its picks; and what they resolved to). Feats, spells, invocations, option picks, masteries, expertise, the skills and ability increases feats gave, the subclass and maximum hit points are rebuilt from the records whenever one changes; damage taken carries over. Items from before records existed are kept as they are.
    - **Short rest** is a menu: **Spend a hit die** (shows how many are left of the total, one per level, the class's die; rolls it plus the CON modifier through the dice parser and heals that much) and **Finish short rest** (brings pact slots back).
    - **Long rest** restores hit points, clears temporary hit points and death saves, brings every slot back and regains half the hit dice (at least one).
  - **Saving**: every change is saved on its own about half a second after it's made, and right away when leaving the sheet. There is no Save button; the top bar shows Unsaved changes / Saving… / Saved, or the error with **Retry**. A save only replaces the file once it's valid; the id and creation time never change.
  - **Read-only**: a character in an archived campaign opens with a banner and every control disabled until the campaign is restored.
  - **Header card**: portrait (clicking it opens the picture at its original size in a popup: the full-size picture kept beside the file, else the small one in the file, else the class art; Escape, ✕ or a click outside closes it), name, "Species · Class Level · Background · Played by", and the conditions as chips with ✕, plus **+ Add condition** (a menu of the conditions in the rules data). On the right:
    - **Speed** (the species' walking speed) and a **Heroic Inspiration** toggle; while on, the portrait glows gold. A long rest turns it on when one of the character's features, traits or feats says so ("You gain Heroic Inspiration whenever you finish a Long Rest": the Human's Resourceful); the rest notification names it.
    - The AC, Init and PB boxes are labelled with their abbreviation and an icon (shield, lightning bolt, star); the full name shows on hover and is what screen readers hear.
    - **Armor class** in a shield shape. 10 + DEX without armor; light armor adds DEX, medium adds DEX up to +2, heavy adds none; an equipped shield adds its bonus. Clicking the number sets it by hand (Unarmored Defense, magic armor); empty goes back to computed.
    - **Initiative** (DEX modifier) and **Proficiency** bonus.
    - **Hit points**: current / max, **Temp** (clicking it sets temporary hit points to the amount; they replace, don't stack), an amount field with a red **−** (damage: temporary hit points first, never below 0, damage at 0 is a failed death save) and a green **+** (heal up to max), and a bar showing how much is left, shading from green at full to red as they run out. At 0 hit points the numbers give way to **Death saves**: three Pass and three Fail circles with the status Dying, Stable or Dead. Healing above 0 clears them.
  - **Left column**: two sub-columns. The first holds the six abilities (full name in bold with the modifier under it, and the score large on the right; two per row), **Saving throws** (proficient ones marked), followed by what the species (or its chosen lineage, e.g. a Dragonborn color or a Tiefling legacy) gives: **Advantage** (save advantages from its trait text, shortened: "Advantage: Charmed condition", "Advantage: INT, WIS, CHA saves"; a sentence with a condition attached, like the Tortle's "Until you emerge…", is shown whole on its own line), then Resistances, Immunities (damage types and conditions on one line) and Vulnerabilities, each only when it has any, gathered from the species (or lineage), feats and items in use (equipped, and attuned when they need it) through their rules data (a choice not settled, like Boon of Energy Resistance's, is left out), and from class and subclass features and species traits by their wording about the character ("You have Resistance to Necrotic damage"; "magic can't put you to sleep", an Elf's Trance, shows as the immunity Magical sleep); each entry's tooltip says where it comes from ("From Tiefling, Ring of Resistance"); **Senses, Proficiencies & languages**: passive Perception, Insight and Investigation, then armor, weapon, tool and language proficiencies from the class, background and species (fixed ones plus the tools and languages picked in the wizard, at levels or through feats; a category without any is left out). The second, a fixed 270 px wide (below the first when there is no room), holds **Skills** with their ability and bonus, proficient ones marked; expertise doubles the proficiency bonus and rings the mark. A skill the character has advantage on shows a green dot with a white A after its name, disadvantage a red dot with a white D. Both come from the text of the species traits, feats, class and subclass features up to the level, and the items in use (equipped, and attuned when they need it), e.g. "Advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks"; armor whose data marks Stealth gives disadvantage on Stealth. Those are fixed: shown without a toggle, the tooltip naming where they come from. A skill without such a source can have either set by hand on the sheet (the A and D toggles show on hover or focus; saved with the character). A skill with both has neither: they cancel out, and the A and D show only as colored outlines with a tooltip saying so. The first sub-column is 270 px wide, the left column only as wide as its two sub-columns; the tabs get the rest.
  - **Tabs** (right column; arrow keys move between them): Actions, Spells, Inventory, Features & traits, Notes & backstory. The sheet opens on Actions.
  - **Time filter**: right under the tab bar, on the Actions, Spells and Features & traits tabs, a row of small toggle buttons: Action, Bonus action, Reaction, Other. None picked shows everything; with one or more picked, only rows used that way show (from their A / BA / R; weapon attacks and the Unarmed Strike are actions; anything else, like a 1-minute casting time or a passive feature, is Other), and a group left empty is hidden. A pool on the Actions tab shows when one of its options passes. The filter is kept while switching between those tabs; when it leaves nothing, the tab says so. Filtering never changes the column widths: Time (56 px), Range (96 px), Hit/DC and Damage (72 px) and Notes have fixed widths (padding included); longer text ends in "…" and shows whole on hover.
  - **Actions tab**: one row per equipped weapon: name (opens the item's rules preview, like spell names; an item's preview lists, when the item has them: type with weapon category and rarity, attunement, reach, range, damage (with the two-handed die for versatile), damage type, armor class, Strength needed, Stealth disadvantage, properties, mastery, weight and cost, then its text), range (or 5 ft reach), attack bonus (STR, DEX for ranged weapons, the better of the two for finesse; proficiency from the class), damage with its type, and its properties ("Mastery: Sap" when the character has that weapon's mastery). A weapon the character is proficient with gets a dark circle with a white P after its name (nothing when not proficient). A weapon the character has mastery in also gets a gold star after its name; hovering (or focusing or clicking) the star shows what the mastery does ("Sap: If you hit a creature with this weapon, …"), from the rules data. The rows are under an **Attacks** group heading, and the features ticked on the Features & traits tab under **Features** (Name, Time, then their uses and rest badge; a ticked feature that draws from a pool is bundled under that pool's row, indented with ↳ and without uses of its own: Channel Divinity with its boxes, then Divine Sense and Sacred Weapon; the pool shows even when only its options are ticked); spells are on the Spells tab, not here. After the weapons comes **Unarmed Strike**: A, 5 ft, Hit/DC STR modifier + proficiency bonus, Damage 1 + STR modifier bludgeoning, the P (everyone is proficient), and Notes "Damage, Grapple, Shove" (what an Unarmed Strike can do). Its name opens the Unarmed Strike rule, which describes the three. A Time column follows the name: A for an attack. Above the table, three lines: **Standard actions:**, **Bonus actions:** and **Reactions:**. Each lists the standard actions taken that way, from the rules data in the book of the character's class (else every book, one per name), alphabetically (Attack, Dash…; Two-Weapon Fighting; Opportunity Attack; varying ones, like Cast a Spell, count as actions; free ones, like End Concentration, are left out; "Improvising an Action" shows as "Improvise"; names separated by ", "), then the character's own features used that way (by their Time, as on the Features & traits tab: Lay on Hands, Cunning Action…), with the standard actions their text names in brackets: "Cunning Action (Dash, Disengage, Hide)". Every name opens its rules in the preview card (a feature's with "From: …"). The lines follow the time filter; a line with nothing on it is left out. The Hit column is called Hit/DC; The Hit/DC and Damage columns are 72 px wide (padding included), their values in boxes of one size, centered (Damage boxes always have room for two lines); Notes is 150 px wide (padding included); Damage and Notes wrap onto a new line.
  - **Spells tab**: spellcasting ability, spell save DC (8 + modifier + proficiency) and spell attack bonus, a **Concentrating** toggle (the header card glows violet while on), and the spell slots: one box per slot per spell level, from the class table, pact magic slots on their own row. Then the character's spells, in the same table design as the spells on the Actions tab (Name, Time, Range, Hit/DC, Damage, Notes; the C square after the name for concentration; Ritual and the components in Notes), but compact (smaller text, less height per row; Time and Range at a fixed width, cut off with "…" and shown whole on hover; Notes 96 px, so the name gets more room) and with every spell and one row group per level (Cantrips, 1st level…), the columns lined up across levels. Hit/DC uses the spell's own ability when it has one (Magic Initiate's pick), else the class's. After the name, the red (!) when the character got the spell from more than one place, listing every place. Before the name, a **Cast** button: it uses a spell slot of the spell's level or higher (never lower; pact slots count too). With one slot level to choose from it casts right away; with several, it opens a small menu of them ("2nd level slot (1 left)"); with none left it is disabled. A feature that casts the spell without a spell slot (Paladin's Smite: Divine Smite once per long rest; Magic Initiate: its own spell) is offered first, "Paladin's Smite (no slot, 1 left)", and casting that way ticks the feature's use box on the Features & traits tab instead of using a slot; with no free cast or slot left, Cast is disabled. A cantrip uses no slot. Casting a concentration spell turns Concentrating on and that spell's row glows like the header card; casting another moves the glow, and turning Concentrating off ends it. Concentration also ends on its own when the character finishes a short rest or takes a long rest (the notification says so: "Concentration on Bless ended."). Taking damage doesn't end it: the character makes a saving throw to keep it, and the DM turns Concentrating off if it fails. A notification says what was cast and with which slot. A spell added outside Level up has ✕ to remove it. Every listed spell is prepared or known (there is no prepared box); "Prepared: n of N" counts the leveled class spells against the class table. Spells come from Level up.
  - **Inventory tab**: coin boxes (CP, SP, EP, GP, PP; whole numbers, centered, each with a − / + row joined underneath it as one block; − without a coin of that kind breaks the smallest bigger coin (0 cp and 3 sp: − on CP gives 9 cp and 2 sp; change in gp, sp and cp) and is only greyed out when there's nothing to break; the coins, carrying bar and the rest stay on one line), carried weight against the carrying capacity (STR × 15 lb, doubled per size above Medium) with a bar, attuned items out of 3, and an add bar, always shown (not in an archived campaign), with one search field (the item catalog loads when it's first focused): the matching catalog items (limited to the campaign's sourcebooks) float below it with their price, and a last row **Add "…" as custom item** takes what's typed as an item of its own. Picking a row fills the field without adding anything; right of the field, **Add** puts it in the inventory and **Buy (15 gp)** does so and pays from the coins (small coins first; a bigger coin is broken and the change comes back in gp, sp and cp; disabled without a price or with too few coins, "Not enough coins"); a notification confirms a purchase. Typing again clears the pick; adding an item that's already there adds a separate line (two Daggers can each be equipped and each get an attack row); the + button raises a line's quantity. The table: Equip (with a small grip before the box, shown when the row is hovered or the grip focused: drag it onto another row to move the item there, or use the arrow keys; the order is saved), item (the name opens the item's window: for a rules item its full rules entry plus quantity, weight, price and equipped/attuned; for a custom item a form to change its name, quantity, weight, price, attunement, armor type and AC or shield bonus, weapon type, damage and damage type, finesse, ranged and range, and a description, saved as you go; no book abbreviation), quantity (a number field with stacked + and − buttons to its right; − stops at 0, which keeps the item listed and its Equipped tick but counts as unequipped: no armor class from it, no attack row; ✕ removes it), weight, price (per item, from the rules data, in the fewest coins: "15 gp", "2 sp 5 cp"; custom items have none), and notes (armor class, weapon damage, and Attuned for items that need it, at most 3 at once).
  - **Features & traits tab**: the same compact table as the Spells tab, in groups: **Class features** (class and subclass features up to the character's level, by level; the subclass's replace the "Subclass" placeholder; a feature its parent's text refers to as an entry of its own, like Divine Sense in Channel Divinity or Sacred Weapon in Oath of Devotion, gets its own row right after the parent, except the options of a choice), **Species traits**, **Feats** and **Other picks** (invocations and the like, with their target: "Agonizing Blast (Eldritch Blast)"). Columns: a checkbox (ticked: the feature also shows on the Actions tab), Name (its preview card, or a species trait's unfolded text, starts with where it comes from: "From: Paladin 3", "From: Feat · Human"; a pick shows after it, "Chosen: Protector"; clicking the name opens its preview card, or for a species trait, which has no entry of its own, unfolds its text under the row), Time (A, BA or R, read from its text), Uses and Rest. **Uses**: when the text says how often it can be used, boxes to tick off (a big pool, like Lay On Hands, is a −/+ counter of what's left); the count comes from the class table (Channel Divinity, Rages, Second Wind), else the text (an ability modifier, the proficiency bonus, once, twice, or a pool of so many times the level). A feature that recharges but doesn't say how often gets a number field to set its uses by hand. **Rest** shows a clear Short rest or Long rest badge for a feature whose uses come back; a short rest gives back one use or all, as its text says, and a long rest gives back everything (the rest notifications say so). Where it all comes from, first that wins: (1) what's filled in by hand in the feature's preview card, (2) the rules data itself (`consumes`: the pool a feature spends, e.g. Turn Undead and Divine Spark draw from Channel Divinity; `additionalSpells` with daily/rest uses: a free cast of those spells, once per long or short rest), (3) the wording of its text. A feature that draws from a pool shows and ticks that pool's boxes. **Usage section** in the preview card of a class or subclass feature, feat or other pick: one line with how it's used ("Action · 2 uses · short rest: gives back one · draws from Channel Divinity"), marked "set by hand" or "detected", and **Edit**: usage (none, uses, pool, free spell cast and its spell), number of uses (a number, an ability modifier, the proficiency bonus, N × class level, or a class table column), short rest (gives back all, one or nothing), long rest, time, range, damage, saving throw and draws from (a feature name); each field starts at "Detected: …" and only what's changed is saved. It's kept app-wide (feature-usage.json in the app data folder, not touched by a rules re-import), so it's filled in once for every character; a class or subclass feature is kept per class (and subclass), since several share a name: the Cleric's and the Paladin's Channel Divinity are separate. A feature's name opens that exact entry, never another class's of the same name. On the Actions tab, a feature with a range, damage or saving throw fills those columns (the save as "WIS 13", with the class's spell save DC), its uses and rest in Notes.
  - **Notes & backstory tab**: Personality traits, Ideals, Bonds, Flaws and Backstory.
  - States: loading, not found (with Back to Characters), a load error with Retry, no rules data (tracking works; numbers from the class and species show "—").

### 05 · LegendKeeper reader
- **Header bar**: world name, connection and sync status, Refresh, Connection settings.
- **Page tree**: a filter, pinned pages, then the full folder hierarchy.
- **Read-only page view**
  - Breadcrumb and a "Read only" badge.
  - Buttons: Pin, Show image on player screen, **Open in LegendKeeper ↗**.
  - Links between pages open inside the app.
- **GM secret blocks** are visible here but must **never** be sent to the player display.
- **Side panel**: page properties, linked app items (for example encounters), and backlinks.

### 06 · Player display (second window)
- A separate full-screen window on display 2, with a dark theme readable from across the table.
- **Content**
  - Round, whose turn it is, and who is up next.
  - The initiative list.
  - A shared handout or image area.
- **What players see**
  - PCs show exact HP.
  - **Monsters show status words only** (Unhurt, Bloodied, Down) plus their conditions.
- **Control**: entirely from the DM screen. The DM can blank it at any time.

## Cross-cutting behaviour

- **Dev and installed app**: the development build keeps its own data folder, separate from the installed app, and its window title says "(dev)".
- **Linking**
  - Encounters link to LegendKeeper pages.
  - Search results show the lore pages that mention them.
  - LegendKeeper pages show the encounters linked to them.
- **Pins**: anything can be pinned to the current session.
- **Keyboard**: no global shortcuts for search or for switching screens. Space advances to the next turn.
- **Storage**: characters, encounters, notes and settings are stored locally, per campaign. A character without a campaign is stored app-wide.
- **Notifications**: short confirmations (a character copied or deleted, a rest taken, a hit die spent) pop up in the bottom-right corner of the DM window. Only one shows at a time; a new one replaces it. It goes away after 5 seconds, or when clicked. Errors stay next to the control that failed.
- **Window size**: the main window opens at the size it had when it was last closed (and maximized if it was), fitted to the current screen. It appears only once it is ready, so the resize is never visible.
